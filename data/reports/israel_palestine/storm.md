# summary


**Israel and Palestine Should Pursue a Long-Term Joint Economic Zone to Promote Regional Stability**  
A long-term joint economic zone between Israel and Palestine has been proposed as a means to foster sustainable development, reduce inequality, and build a foundation for lasting regional stability. The concept envisions a shared industrial and commercial area where Israeli and Palestinian enterprises operate cooperatively under joint governance, infrastructure, and investment frameworks[1][2]. Advocates argue that such a zone could transform existing patterns of economic asymmetry—marked by Palestinian dependence on Israeli markets and restrictions on trade—into a partnership grounded in mutual prosperity and peacebuilding[3][4][5].  
The proposal arises from a century-long history of economic and political interdependence deeply intertwined with the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Since the mid-20th century, economic disparities and restricted access to resources in the Palestinian territories have contributed to widespread poverty and unemployment, while Israel’s high-tech and manufacturing sectors have continued to expand[3][6][7]. Proponents of an integrated economic zone contend that bridging this divide through shared industrial parks, modern infrastructure, and cross-border collaboration could address the structural roots of instability and create incentives for peace[1][8].  
Various economic models and case studies—such as the Barkan Industrial Park and Gaza Industrial Estate—have demonstrated the potential and challenges of combined Israeli–Palestinian ventures[9][10]. Successful implementation would require equitable resource allocation, transparent governance mechanisms, and coordinated development strategies that ensure fair distribution of benefits. International organizations and regional initiatives, including those promoted under the Abraham Accords, have underscored that cross-border economic integration can complement political negotiations by creating tangible shared interests[11][12].  
Despite its promise, the idea of a joint economic zone remains politically contentious. Supporters describe it as a pragmatic, peace-oriented framework for addressing economic inequality and rebuilding trust, while critics warn that it could normalize existing power imbalances or reinforce occupation dynamics if not structured under equal partnership and clear legal frameworks[13][14]. Balancing these concerns, scholars and policymakers emphasize that an effectively designed joint economic zone—anchored in transparency, parity, and cooperation—could serve as a cornerstone for both long-term economic resilience and a more stable coexistence between Israel and Palestine[5][15][8].


# Background


The economic and political relationship between Israel and the Palestinians has been shaped by over a century of conflict and territorial disputes. The roots of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict can be traced to the late 19th century, when the Zionist movement sought to establish a national homeland for the Jewish people in Palestine, a region that was then under Ottoman rule and inhabited primarily by Arab communities[16]. Following the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948 and the subsequent Arab–Israeli wars, tensions escalated between the two peoples over borders, sovereignty, and the rights of displaced populations[17][18].
Decades of hostilities and intermittent peace processes have deeply affected the region’s economic landscape. Palestinian territories, particularly Gaza and the West Bank, have suffered from restricted movement, trade barriers, and limited access to resources[3][19]. The dependence of the Palestinian economy on Israel remains significant; around 90 percent of Palestinian exports are directed toward Israeli markets, and key inputs for production are heavily reliant on Israeli supply chains[4]. Historically, many Palestinians worked inside Israel, with employment levels reaching roughly 50 percent of Gaza’s workforce in the mid-1980s, reflecting a high degree of labor interdependence[20].
However, cycles of conflict and political instability have repeatedly disrupted economic activity. Israeli security measures and regional wars, including recent clashes with Iran, have damaged infrastructure and reduced investment opportunities in both economies[21]. In the Palestinian territories, economic collapse has been further exacerbated by the withholding of fiscal transfers, declining external support, and sharp declines in GDP—such as the World Bank’s estimated 26 percent contraction in 2024[6][22]. This deterioration has heightened unemployment and poverty, widening the economic gap between Israelis and Palestinians[3][5].
In contrast, Israel’s economy has continued to grow, supported by advancements in technology, agriculture, and industrial development. The Israeli government has expanded industrial zones, such as the Tekoma park, which created thousands of new jobs and bolstered economic growth in southern regions[7][23]. Proponents of economic cooperation argue that similar joint industrial initiatives between Israel and Palestine—centered on shared production and equitable taxation mechanisms—could serve as pragmatic steps toward regional stability and mutual prosperity[1].


# Concept of a Joint Economic Zone


A joint economic zone between Israel and Palestine is envisioned as a collaborative industrial, commercial, and logistical area designed to stimulate bilateral economic growth while fostering stability and cooperation in the region. The core idea is to establish shared industrial parks or zones where both Israeli and Palestinian businesses can operate under an integrated framework that supports mutual economic interdependence[1][24].


## Structure and Objectives


The proposed joint economic zone would combine key sectors such as manufacturing, technology, agriculture, and tourism, creating a shared space with modern infrastructure and facilities that help lower operational costs for both parties[2][15]. By offering unified industrial infrastructure, the initiative aims to bridge economic disparities, promote employment, and provide a sustainable peace-oriented framework to engage both economies in cooperative development[5][8].


## Historical and Policy Background


Industrial zones in Israel and the Palestinian territories have existed historically, often under circumstances of economic segregation and uneven access to resources. Reports have documented Israeli industrial zones constructed on occupied land, underscoring the need for equitable economic arrangements[13][19]. The concept of a joint economic zone reimagines these models in a manner that integrates both economies as equal partners rather than as competing or dependent entities[25].


## Governance and Integration


Successful implementation depends on cooperative governance mechanisms, possibly through joint management or shared municipal coordination, as seen in earlier industrial park initiatives in Israel that relied on inter-municipal collaboration and tax redistribution policies[23][25]. Long-term leasing or co-administrative arrangements have been recommended to ensure the joint zone operates transparently and with balanced participation from both sides.


## Role in Peacebuilding and Regional Stability


Proponents of the joint economic zone argue that economic inequity lies at the heart of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and that collaboration through shared industrial and commercial ventures could serve as a foundation for peace[5]. The mutual benefits of such a zone—providing shelter, jobs, and stability—align with broader goals of regional integration and peaceful coexistence[8]. By framing economic cooperation as a “game changer,” this approach emphasizes economic interdependence as a pathway to sustained stability and reconciliation[1][24].


# Economic Rationale and Potential Impact


Economic cooperation between Israel and Palestine has long been viewed as an essential component of achieving durable peace and regional stability. Persistent economic inequality between the two sides has been identified as a key driver of tension, with Palestinian economic life having deteriorated significantly over the past decades due to structural barriers, restrictions on movement, and limited access to markets[5][19]. These inequalities have worsened the investment climate, constrained development, and heightened unemployment and poverty across the Palestinian territories[3][6].
A joint economic zone would aim to address such disparities by fostering mutual economic interests and shared growth. Reports suggest that economic equity must be incorporated into any lasting peace plan, as sustainable stability is unlikely without narrowing the income and opportunity gap between Israelis and Palestinians[5]. Establishing joint industrial zones and integrating Palestinian production into regional supply chains could help diversify the Palestinian economy, increase foreign investment, and boost job creation[15][2][14].
The Palestinian Authority (PA) has previously implemented export-oriented industrial zones, supported by international donors, to stimulate production and trade in targeted sectors[14]. These zones, if expanded and managed collaboratively with Israel, could provide modern infrastructure, reduce business costs, and attract private investment across borders[2][1]. The regional model of industrial collaboration—such as that between Egypt and Israel, which enabled competitively priced exports to the United States—illustrates how joint ventures can enhance efficiency and global market access[26].
The integration of economic networks could also create new employment opportunities, addressing the long-term decline in Palestinian labor participation in Israel, particularly from Gaza, where half of the workforce was once employed across the border before restrictions became severe[20][4]. By allowing for shared industrial operations, the proposed zone could generate common benefits, including employment, technological exchange, and improved infrastructure[8][1].
Israel’s advanced technological and industrial base, combined with the Palestinian workforce’s potential and geographic advantage, presents opportunities for complementary growth rather than competition[27][4]. Moreover, bilateral trade already demonstrates the scale of interdependence, with total trade volume reaching $3.24 billion in 2024, signaling that both economies remain deeply interconnected despite ongoing conflict[28].
In the long term, a well-structured joint economic zone could help mitigate revenue shortfalls, stabilize fiscal conditions, and reduce reliance on volatile external aid[6]. It could also pave the way for broader economic integration at the regional and global levels[15]. By fostering a shared stake in prosperity, such collaboration offers a pragmatic economic foundation for peacebuilding between Israel and Palestine[8][1].


# Sectoral Focus of Cooperation


## Tourism and Cultural Exchange


Tourism constitutes a further sector capable of generating mutual growth. Shared economic zones that foster a stable political climate could encourage regional tourism and cultural exchange, drawing on attractions and religious sites that are significant to multiple communities[15]. Such cooperation could stimulate hospitality industries and cross-border infrastructure, fostering people-to-people engagement essential for lasting peace.


## Industrial and Manufacturing Collaboration


Industrial zones have historically served as a major field for economic cooperation between Israel and its neighbors, offering a model that could be adapted for a long-term joint economic zone. Israel’s experience in inter-municipal industrial park development and tax-sharing arrangements demonstrates potential governance frameworks for such initiatives within a joint Israeli–Palestinian context[23]. The concept of “integrated business” projects—joint ventures involving Israelis and Palestinians working side by side in development zones in areas such as Judea and Samaria—has already provided valuable lessons for shared management, labor integration, and mutual economic benefit[8]. Previous cooperative frameworks, such as the Qualified Industrial Zones (QIZs) between Egypt and Israel, also highlight how cross-border industrial collaboration can enhance productivity and attract international markets[26]. Leveraging Israel’s advanced industrial manufacturing sector could provide technological and logistical foundations for a shared zone[29], while new governance models could ensure equitable distribution of benefits between the parties involved.


## Shared Industrial Zones and Territorial Considerations


Existing Israeli Industrial Zones (IZs) on occupied or disputed territories have raised significant political and ethical questions[13]. For a new joint economic zone to promote genuine cooperation, it would need to be established under mutually agreed terms, ensuring fair access to resources, transparent management, and joint benefit allocation. The focus would ideally shift from unilateral economic control to co-governance, enabling more balanced participation in economic planning and tax redistribution.


## Information and Technology Cooperation


Rapid growth in the information and communication technology (ICT) sector offers another promising area of collaboration. The Palestinian ICT industry is developing steadily, encompassing software development and telecommunications infrastructure[30]. Joint projects with Israeli technology companies have already provided Palestinian software engineers with practical experience, funding, and shared access to global networks[31]. A joint economic zone emphasizing ICT could create high-value employment opportunities, reduce economic dependency, and cultivate long-term professional partnerships.


## Trade and Market Interdependence


Economic interdependence remains a defining feature of Israeli–Palestinian relations: approximately 90 percent of Palestinian exports are directed toward the Israeli market[4]. A formalized joint economic zone could help stabilize and diversify this trade relationship, promoting value-added production and reducing structural vulnerabilities. Through carefully managed sectors that integrate industrial, technological, and cultural development, both economies could advance toward sustainable and cooperative growth.


# Mechanisms of Cooperation


The proposed framework for an Israeli–Palestinian joint economic zone relies on institutional and economic mechanisms that strengthen trust, shared prosperity, and mutual dependency. Organizations such as the Alliance for Peace in the Middle East, headquartered in Washington, D.C., have served as intermediaries to mobilize funding and design trust-building initiatives between Israelis and Palestinians, particularly in the context of business collaboration and local capacity building[11].


## Integrated Business and Joint Ventures


Integrated business models emphasize shared ownership and operation of commercial and industrial ventures between Israelis and Palestinians. These ventures, particularly those located in the West Bank (Judea and Samaria), have demonstrated that interdependence in trade and labor can yield mutual economic benefits and social stability[8][9]. The concept focuses on joint production, export partnerships, and investment frameworks that encourage collaboration across industrial sectors.


## Industrial Zones as Platforms for Cooperation


Israel’s existing industrial zones in the West Bank offer a useful precedent for designing future economic cooperation zones. These areas have already shown potential for symbiotic economic activity where both Palestinian and Israeli workers and entrepreneurs contribute to manufacturing, technology, and service industries[9]. Industrial parks are often built through inter-municipal cooperation, which allows for coordinated planning, shared infrastructure, and equitable taxation between administrative authorities[23]. Such models have been promoted as a pathway away from civil and economic separation toward a joint economic future[1].


## Infrastructure and Cost-Sharing Arrangements


One of the principal mechanisms for effective integration involves the establishment of comprehensive infrastructure systems. Joint industrial zones can provide shared utilities, transport links, and logistical networks that reduce operating costs and enhance export efficiency[2]. These cost-saving measures encourage investment, promote local business development, and improve the economic viability of cooperative projects.


## Economic Environment and Trade Dynamics


Trade between Israel and the Palestinian territories remains significant, reaching $3.24 billion in 2024—a year-on-year increase of 11%, characterized by Israeli exports in technology and imports in gold and jewelry[28]. Despite this growth, the overall investment environment remains constrained by fiscal pressures, revenue shortfalls, and reduced international support[3][6]. A structured economic zone that allows transparent revenue sharing and stable governance mechanisms could mitigate these challenges by creating predictable trade policies and integrated production chains.
In sum, the mechanisms of cooperation center on joint economic frameworks, shared industrial development, and coordinated governance—all of which aim to transform mutual dependence into a durable foundation for peace and regional stability.


# Case Studies of Joint Economic Projects


## Barkan Industrial Park


The Barkan Industrial Park, established near the city of Ariel in the central West Bank, serves as one of the oldest and most prominent examples of integrated Israeli–Palestinian industrial activity[32][9]. Located approximately 25 kilometres east of Tel Aviv, the park hosts a wide range of factories, offices, and small enterprises employing both Israeli and Palestinian workers[32]. Proponents argue that it demonstrates the mutual benefits of economic cooperation by creating employment opportunities and fostering pragmatic engagement between the two populations[9]. Critics, however, have questioned the park’s legal status and the implications of its operation on occupied land[13].


## Atarot Industrial Zone


Atarot Industrial Zone is situated between the Palestinian neighborhood of Bayt Hanina and the Qalandiya checkpoint in East Jerusalem, and is currently recognized as the largest industrial area in that region[33]. Established before the Oslo Accords, Atarot has long been a hub for light manufacturing and warehousing. Despite its size and potential, its expansion and activity have been hindered by political tensions, movement restrictions, and debates over sovereignty in East Jerusalem[33][13].


## Gaza Industrial Estate


The Gaza Industrial Estate, also known as the Karni Industrial Zone, was originally developed under Palestinian oversight with international donor support as a means of boosting Gaza’s export potential and private-sector employment[10][14]. The zone, located southeast of Gaza City, was designed to facilitate trade between Palestinian and Israeli markets while encouraging investment through shared logistical infrastructure[10]. The site’s periodic closures during heightened conflict have, however, hindered its effectiveness, illustrating the vulnerability of joint economic initiatives to political and security developments[3][19].


## Tekoma Industrial Park


In southern Israel, the Tekoma Industrial Park project offers a contemporary model of large-scale cooperative development. Approved by the Israeli government to create approximately 5,000 jobs, the park aims to strengthen regional economies and provide a platform for collaboration between neighboring communities[7][2]. While primarily situated within Israel’s borders, its inclusive employment strategy reflects growing interest in integrating Palestinian labor and businesses into wider regional networks[8][1].


## Evaluation of Inter-Municipal and Cross-Border Cooperation


Israel’s experience with inter-municipal cooperation in industrial zone planning, dating back to initiatives in the early 1990s, demonstrates how shared governance frameworks can enable more equitable distribution of tax revenues and resources[23]. Similar approaches could be applied to future Israeli–Palestinian economic zones to balance benefits across jurisdictions and communities. Scholars and policy experts have argued that such cooperative frameworks promote regional development and offer a foundation for mitigating long-term economic disparities between Israelis and Palestinians[5][15].


# Success Factors and Lessons Learned


## Integrated Business Models


Experiments in joint Israeli–Palestinian business ventures have demonstrated that shared economic goals can foster cooperation even amid political uncertainty. Integrated business initiatives in Judea and Samaria have illustrated that collaboration focusing on core needs such as housing, employment, and social stability can generate mutual benefits and create a framework for coexistence through commerce[8]. These ventures reveal that practical cooperation built on tangible economic outcomes—rather than political alignment—can strengthen community bonds and incentivize peace-oriented behavior.


## Industrial Parks as Catalysts for Stability


The development of industrial zones and parks on shared or adjacent territory has been recognized as a key mechanism for economic integration. Efforts to establish Palestinian industrial parks have sought not only to generate jobs and attract investment but also to support the broader peace process by providing a concrete economic foundation for co-dependence and shared prosperity[24]. Studies of inter-municipal cooperation in Israel’s own industrial park system show that joint governance and fair tax redistribution are essential to ensuring that benefits are equitably shared among participating communities[23]. These lessons suggest that well-designed public–private partnerships and transparent revenue-sharing models are necessary for sustaining the trust essential to long-term joint economic zones.


## The Role of Collaborative Employment and Skill Development


Collaborative employment initiatives between Israelis and Palestinians have created new opportunities for professional growth, particularly for Palestinian software engineers who gain access to real-world experience through Israeli internships and funding partnerships[31]. Such workforce integration strengthens local capacity and facilitates a more balanced exchange of knowledge and technology, helping reduce economic disparities. Ultimately, these programs highlight that cross-border human capital investment yields not only technical expertise but also stronger intersocietal networks.


## Economic Interdependence and the Peace Dividend


Past examples underscore that economic collaboration can shift incentives away from zero-sum politics toward shared growth. Creating joint economies through industrial zones transforms the current separation-based model by demonstrating that security and prosperity are mutually reinforcing[1]. In contrast, interruptions caused by regional conflicts, such as the destruction following the 12-day conflict with Iran and the aftermath of the October 7 attacks, reveal the fragility of isolated or uneven economic systems and underscore the need for diversified, cooperative frameworks that can withstand regional shocks[21][5]. Broader models—like the $10 billion in trade generated under the Abraham Accords—show the positive multiplier effects when political normalization is paired with open markets[12].


## Building Trust and Sustained Engagement


International initiatives, including those led by organizations such as the Alliance for Peace in the Middle East, have demonstrated the importance of consistent funding and facilitation for trust-building projects between Israelis and Palestinians[11]. Long-term success relies not merely on economic instruments but on sustained interpersonal and institutional engagement. Lessons from regional economic integration studies further indicate that connecting the Palestinian economy to broader global networks can reinforce resilience and mutual dependence, ensuring that peacebuilding is not solely local but globally embedded[15]. Together, these experiences reveal that durable joint economic zones require both structural integration and continuous commitment to equitable partnership.


# Challenges and Obstacles


Efforts to establish a long-term joint economic zone between Israel and Palestine face a number of political, economic, and structural challenges. One of the most significant obstacles is the longstanding economic inequity between the two sides, which many experts argue must be addressed for any sustainable peace initiative to succeed[5]. Restrictions on Palestinian economic activity, particularly in areas under Israeli control, have hampered development and deepened disparities. Severe constraints on access to mines, quarries, and other natural resources in Area C have limited Palestinian producers’ ability to extract materials and engage in large-scale manufacturing[3].
The overall investment climate has also suffered due to recurrent instability and conflict. Periodic escalations of hostilities have caused extensive damage to infrastructure and disrupted economic activity across both territories[21][19]. These cyclical crises contribute to persistent unemployment and poverty among Palestinians while simultaneously impacting Israeli industries that rely on shared labor and supply chains[5].
Politically, the proposed joint economic projects are often viewed through competing lenses. Some proponents view industrial zones as essential tools to link economies and foster mutual dependence that could bolster the peace process[24][34][1]. Critics, however, contend that such initiatives may entrench the existing political order by normalizing the occupation and reinforcing unequal relations between Israelis and Palestinians[14].
Beyond political perceptions, structural hurdles complicate the implementation of joint ventures. Unequal access to permits, land, and essential infrastructure continues to disadvantage Palestinian producers, who face higher operating costs and limited market access compared to their Israeli counterparts[19][2]. These issues collectively highlight the complex interplay between economic design, political realities, and security concerns in attempts to create a shared economic space that can serve as a foundation for long-term regional stability.


# Implementation Strategy for a Long-Term Zone


The implementation of a long-term Israeli–Palestinian joint economic zone relies on an approach that merges economic cooperation with governance frameworks capable of sustaining mutual benefits and stability.


## Integrated Infrastructure and Investment Incentives


A central component of the proposed strategy involves establishing industrial zones featuring integrated infrastructure and logistics systems designed to streamline business operations and reduce costs[2]. Such zones would include facilities for manufacturing, transport, and trade under a unified management structure. To attract both domestic and international investment, these areas would offer incentives such as tax benefits and simplified bureaucratic procedures[2].


## Joint Management and Governance Framework


The Israeli–Palestinian industrial park model envisions the creation of a jointly managed economic area governed by representatives from both sides, potentially structured under a long-term lease agreement[25]. This arrangement aims to ensure parity in decision-making and oversight over land use, labor policies, and security measures. The governance system could draw on inter-municipal cooperation models, focusing on transparent mechanisms for tax redistribution and equitable public service provision[23].


## Transitioning from Separation to Shared Economic Infrastructure


Currently, the dominant dynamic is one of civil separation maintained by security constraints; however, proponents argue that a joint economy operating within industrial zones can serve as a major shift from this status quo[1]. By fostering shared commercial interests, the zone could help reduce tensions and reorient policy toward mutual economic dependence rather than territorial contention.


## Economic and Social Objectives


The broader economic strategy emphasizes fulfilling shared human needs for shelter, employment, and stability, thereby transforming the zone into a platform for peace-oriented engagement rather than competition[8]. The development of Palestinian industrial parks has been explicitly tied to advancing the peace process through improved border cooperation and expanded economic opportunity[24].


## Security and Technological Considerations


While technological developments—including surveillance and monitoring tools—could be leveraged to enhance security and operational efficiency within the zone, debates continue over their ethical implications and trust-building potential[35]. Implementing balanced security measures that protect both populations without impeding economic integration will be crucial for the zone’s legitimacy and sustainability.
